If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please do not rely on this app. Talk to someone now. In South Africa, the SADAG Suicide Crisis Helpline is 0800 567 567 (24 hours), or SMS 31393 and they will call you back; Lifeline South Africa is 0861 322 322; emergencies are 10111, or 112 from a mobile.

Elsewhere: Samaritans on 116 123 in the UK and Ireland, or call or text 988 in the US and Canada. Anywhere at all, your local emergency number.

Testimony Journal is a private place to write. Nothing in it — including scripture the app looks up, prompts, streaks or anything else it shows you — is medical, psychological, pastoral, legal or financial advice, and none of it is a substitute for a qualified professional or your own church community.

This app cannot see what you write, cannot tell when you are struggling, and will never alert anyone on your behalf. It is a notebook, and a notebook cannot help you.

Privacy

What the app holds

  • Your journal entries, and the photos and voice notes inside them.
  • Your prayer requests, milestones, habit ticks and your written story.
  • The tags you create, and the scripture references you type.
  • If you sign in with Google: your name, email address and profile picture — nothing else from your Google account.
  • If you sign in with Apple: your email address, and your name only if you choose to share it. Apple lets you hide your real address behind a relay instead, and the app works exactly the same either way — it never needs to know who you really are.

There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDK watching how you use the app. Your data is never sold and never used to train anything.

Religious belief is sensitive information

A journal about faith reveals your religious beliefs, and the law treats that as needing more care than an ordinary email address. In South Africa, POPIA (the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013) calls it special personal information and prohibits processing it (section 26) unless you have consented (section 27(1)(a)). The UK and EU GDPR say much the same thing in Article 9.

Your consent is the whole basis for this app. By choosing to write in it you are consenting to it storing what you write, for the single purpose of showing it back to you. You can withdraw that consent at any moment by deleting your data — the button at the bottom of the app's Terms & Privacy screen does exactly that, and it does not ask anyone's permission.

Where it lives

If you skipped sign-in, everything stays on your phone and is never sent anywhere. Deleting the app deletes it all.

If you signed in, a copy is also stored in a private account hosted on Supabase. Row-level security means the account can only ever return your own rows; photos and recordings sit in private storage that is read through short-lived signed links rather than public URLs. Traffic is encrypted in transit. It is not end-to-end encrypted: as with any hosted service, someone with administrative access to the database could in principle read what is stored there. If that matters to you, use the app without signing in.

Those servers are outside South Africa. POPIA section 72 restricts sending personal information across the border, so this is said plainly rather than buried: signing in means your journal is stored on infrastructure in another country, and your consent to that is part of choosing to sign in. Skipping sign-in keeps everything on your phone and sends nothing anywhere.

Who else is involved

  • Supabase — hosts the database, files and sign-in for signed-in users.
  • Google — only if you choose “Continue with Google”, and only to confirm who you are.
  • Apple — only if you choose “Continue with Apple”, and only to confirm who you are.
  • bible-api.com — when you type a scripture reference, that reference (and nothing else from your writing) is sent to this free public service to fetch the verse.
  • Your phone's own share sheet — used only when you tap share, and only with what you chose to share.

Permissions

The camera, photo library and microphone are used only at the moment you tap the relevant button, and only to attach what you capture to an entry. Nothing records in the background. You can refuse or revoke any of them in your phone's settings and the rest of the app carries on working.

How long it is kept

Until you delete it. There is no automatic expiry, because a journal you kept for ten years should still be there in the eleventh.

You may also write to connorpace14@gmail.com for access, correction, erasure or a copy of your data, and you will get a reply within 30 days. POPIA gives you these rights directly — access under section 23, correction or deletion under section 24 — and they are not conditional on asking nicely.

If you believe your information has been mishandled, you can complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa), which supervises POPIA, or to your own national authority if you are elsewhere — the ICO in the UK, or your member-state authority in the EU.

Deleting your account

Open the app, tap Terms · Privacy · Your data at the bottom of the Home screen, and use Delete my account. It removes every entry, photo, voice note, prayer, milestone, habit record and your story — from the phone, from the account, and the sign-in record itself. It cannot be undone. If anything cannot be reached (usually because the phone was offline), the app says so plainly rather than claiming a clean wipe, and you can email the address above to have the rest removed.
